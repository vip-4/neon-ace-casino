02 — The basics

Four moves to your first hand.

Blackjack is a comparison game, not a race to 21. Get closer to 21 than the dealer without going over, and the hand is yours.

01

Choose your wager

Pick a chip from 10 to 100. Every new table starts with 1,000 virtual chips. Your balance is saved in this browser, so you can close the tab and come back later.

02

Read the table

Number cards are worth their face value, face cards are 10 and an ace counts as 1 or 11. An ace plus a ten-value card is a blackjack, which pays 3:2.

03

Play your hand

Hit takes another card. Stand keeps your total. Double doubles the bet for one final card. Split turns a matching pair into two hands.

04

Settle and repeat

The dealer reveals the hole card and stands on every 17. Win, lose or push, the result is settled immediately. Press Deal again for the next hand.

i

Nothing here costs money. Neon Ace is a practice game with virtual chips, no accounts and no cash prizes. The only thing at stake is your session score.

03 — House rules

The exact rules in play.

Casino blackjack has dozens of local variations. These are the precise rules this dealer follows, so strategy advice and outcomes line up.

Neon Ace blackjack rules and table settings
Shoe Six standard 52-card decks (312 cards), shuffled in your browser with a cryptographically seeded Fisher–Yates shuffle.
Reshuffle point When fewer than roughly 25% of the shoe remains. The shoe is rebuilt at the end of a hand, never in the middle of one.
Dealer soft 17 Stands on all 17s (S17), including soft 17. The dealer does not take another card on 17.
Blackjack payout 3:2 for an ace plus a ten-value card in the first two cards. A 25-chip blackjack returns 37.5 chips in profit.
Dealer peek When the upcard is an ace or ten-value card, the dealer checks for blackjack before you act. A dealer natural ends the round immediately.
Hit and stand Hit as many times as you like. Stand at any point. A total above 21 busts and loses the wager.
Double down Allowed on any first two cards, including after a split. You receive exactly one more card, then the hand stands automatically.
Split Split two cards of the same value into up to four hands. Split aces receive one card each and cannot be resplit.
Split blackjack A 21 made after splitting pays 1:1, not 3:2. It is a strong total, not a natural blackjack.
Insurance Not offered. There is no side wager when the dealer shows an ace.
Surrender Not offered. You cannot forfeit half your bet to exit a hand.
Push Equal totals return your original wager. A player and dealer natural also pushes.
Dealer draw After all player hands are complete, the dealer hits until reaching 17 or more. If every player hand busts, the dealer reveals the hole card but does not draw.

Why these rules?

This is a common six-deck, stand-on-soft-17 ruleset. It keeps the strategy approachable while staying close to a real casino table.

  • 3:2 matters. A 6:5 blackjack payout quietly raises the house edge. Neon Ace uses the traditional 3:2.
  • S17 is simpler. The dealer never draws on soft 17, so the table is easier to learn.
  • No insurance. The side bet is omitted to keep decisions focused on the hand.
  • No surrender. Every hand is played to a hit, stand, double or split.

Payouts can include half-chips (for example, 37.5 on a 25-chip natural). Balances are displayed with up to two decimal places.

04 — Play better

Basic strategy, without the wall chart.

Basic strategy is the mathematically best play for every hand and dealer upcard. You do not need to memorise a grid — start with the rules that come up most often.

Let the dealer's upcard lead

The dealer's face-up card is the only information that changes your decision. A weak upcard (2 through 6) means the dealer is more likely to bust. A strong upcard (7 through ace) means you need to be more aggressive about reaching a high total.

The hard totals worth memorising

  • 8 or less: always hit. There is no risk of busting.
  • 9, 10 or 11: double against the dealer ranges shown in the chart. Otherwise, hit.
  • 12: stand against 4, 5 or 6; hit against everything else.
  • 13 to 16: stand against 2 through 6; hit against 7 or higher.
  • 17 or more: always stand. The risk of busting outweighs the upside.

Pairs and soft hands

Always split aces and eights. Never split tens or fives. A soft hand contains an ace counted as 11: with A,7, double against 3 through 6, stand against 2, 7 and 8, and hit against 9, 10 or an ace. These three rules cover most of the edge you can gain.

What the house edge actually means

With perfect basic strategy, the house edge on this six-deck, S17, double-after-split ruleset is roughly 0.4%. That is a long-run average, not a promise about your next session. Short sessions are dominated by variance, and no betting pattern can make a negative-expectation game positive.

Keep it in proportion

Treat the bankroll as a score, not a budget. Flat betting and a fixed stop point keep the session comfortable. Chasing losses is the fastest way to turn a practice game into a frustrating one. If gambling stops feeling like entertainment, step away and use the support link in the footer.

05 — Questions

Answers before you ante.

The short version: free, private, virtual chips only. Here are the details people ask about most.

Is Neon Ace really free?

Yes. The game is free to play, has no ads, no in-app purchases and no account requirement. There is no paid tier or hidden wagering.

Can I win real money?

No. Chips are virtual, have no cash value and cannot be deposited or withdrawn. Neon Ace is an entertainment and practice game, not a gambling service. If gambling is affecting your life, support is available at BeGambleAware.org.

Do I need to install anything?

No. It is a single static web page. Open it in a current version of Chrome, Safari, Firefox or Edge and press Deal. There is no app, plug-in or account.

Does it work on a phone?

Yes. The table is responsive, the controls are touch-sized and the layout works in portrait on modern phones. Keyboard shortcuts are a bonus for desktop players.

Is the shuffle fair?

The shoe is shuffled locally with a Fisher–Yates shuffle driven by the Web Crypto API, then reshuffled at roughly 75% penetration. It is designed for fair practice, not for regulated or audited gambling.

Does the game save my progress?

Your bankroll, sound setting and session stats are saved in your browser's local storage. The game sets no cookies and runs no analytics. Use the Reset control to clear the saved data.

What blackjack rules are used?

Six decks, dealer stands on all 17s, blackjack pays 3:2, double on any first two cards including after a split, and up to four hands. Insurance and surrender are not offered. The full rules table has every detail.

Can I count cards?

You can track cards mentally, but the shoe reshuffles at about 75% penetration and there is no cash prize. Neon Ace is a place to practise decisions, not an advantage-play target.

Is the game accessible?

Yes — keyboard shortcuts, visible focus, screen-reader labels on every card, live status announcements, a reduced-motion mode and body text that targets WCAG 2.2 AA contrast. See the accessibility notes.

Do I need to create an account?

No. There is no sign-up, email capture or profile. Your virtual bankroll lives only in this browser, which also means clearing site data or switching devices starts you fresh.

06 — Behind the table

Small game. Deliberate choices.

Neon Ace is a counterpoint to heavyweight casino sites: one page, no trackers, no downloads, and a game loop that starts in under a second after the page loads.

From shortlist to table

The concept came from a screening of open-source and jam-scale browser games. The strongest projects had three things in common: instant play, a ruleset that fits on one screen, and feedback that arrives immediately. Blackjack was the right fit — deep enough to reward strategy, small enough to run as a single static page.

The implementation is original HTML, CSS and vanilla JavaScript. There are no frameworks, remote fonts, tracking scripts or third-party embeds. The only network request is for the page itself and its local assets; after that, every hand is dealt on your device.

Accessibility is part of the game

Cards expose their rank and suit to assistive technology, score changes are announced through a polite live region, and every action is a real button. The table can be played with the keyboard, focus is always visible, and animations collapse when the operating system requests reduced motion. There are no time limits or flashing effects.

What the game does not do

It does not take payments, offer real-money gambling, sell chips, show ads, create accounts or send gameplay data to a server. Virtual chips have no cash value. The game is intended for adults who enjoy card-game strategy and for anyone curious about how blackjack decisions work.

Rules accuracy and review

The dealer follows the six-deck S17 ruleset documented above. Casino rules vary, so a hand played here may resolve differently at another table. This page is reviewed with each release; the update date at the top reflects the last rules or copy review.

Accessibility statement

Target: WCAG 2.2 AA for the page and game controls. Support includes keyboard operation, visible focus, semantic landmarks, screen-reader labels, reduced motion and no time-limited interactions. If you encounter a barrier, the source is available for inspection and improvement.
